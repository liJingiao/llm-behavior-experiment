````text
       For each of the 252 names, the LLM was prompted to write a short, 100-word

biography. To ensure that the model projected characteristics onto the protagonist

based on their name, we instructed it to take on the role of a novelist adept at selecting

names that correspond with specific character traits. Biographies were required to

positive and negative personality traits. Biography generation prompts, along with an

example pair of Jewish and non-Jewish biographies, can be found in the Supplemental

Step 3: Psychological Characteristic Ratings


       In this step, all references to religion and the names themselves were removed

from the biographies. GPT was then prompted to rate various psychological

characteristics of the protagonists based on their biographies, using single-item

questions and Likert scales. These trait inferences reflect the internal associations

GPT has with the content it generated for Jewish exemplars. The rating generation

prompt, along with an example pair of Jewish and non-Jewish character ratings, can

be found in Table S1 of the Supplemental Materials.
````

````text
       GPT and DeepSeek data were analyzed using a series of Bayesian paired

samples t-tests, using the default prior on Cohen’s d of Cauchy (0, 0.707) (Morey &

Rouder, 2024). For each comparison, we report Bayes factors (BF₁₀), as well as the

posterior medians and 95% credible intervals (CIs) of both the raw and standardized

differences (Cohen’s d). For the DeepSeek replication, we also computed a replication

Bayes factor (Ly et al., 2019), which quantifies the relative support the DeepSeek data

provide for the alternative versus the null hypothesis, given the observed GPT data.

This approach was selected to account for the paired structure of the data—Jewish

and non-Jewish biographies matched by age and gender—and to yield a continuous,

probabilistic interpretation of the strength of evidence, which informed the subsequent

analysis.
````
