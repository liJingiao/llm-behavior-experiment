## F01 · 依据1 · §3

原文LF行：181–195。

````text
Value annotation and validation: Refined cases are annotated with directional value tags. A
rule-based validator enforces four structural constraints on the tag matrix, followed by LLM-based
validation of value clarity. Blinded interdisciplinary review: A first physician reviewer evaluates
each LLM-generated case and may approve, edit, or reject it. A second, blinded physician reviewer
then independently approves or rejects the case.

Eliciting decisions. Decision-makers are presented vignettes vi and required to select exactly
one of {c1i , c2i }, presented in randomized order. Value annotations, Ti , are withheld to ensure
that clinical judgment is not confounded by explicit ethical labels. LLMs are instructed to make
recommendations as the persona of an experienced physician advising their patient (Appendix D.1).
The model responds in free-form text, preserving the ecological validity of clinical deliberation, and a
secondary LLM parses the decision. Structured output parsing could short-circuit reasoning and alter
the decision itself. Each model is queried ten times per case at temperature 1.0. Twenty physicians
completed a blinded online survey, making one recommendation in each of the 50 cases, without
access to other respondents’ answers. Full details in Appendix D.2.
````

## F02 · 依据1 · §4

````text
4   Language models are consistent but not pluralistic decision-makers
Models are consistent clinical decision-makers. We measure the binary Shannon entropy of each
model’s repeated decisions per case (10 queries at temperature 1.0) and find that 11 of 12 models have
a median decision entropy of zero. Six models, including Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3 Pro Preview,
and GPT 5.2, zero decision entropy at the 75th percentile. Across models, a median of 82% and
a 75th percentile of 86% of cases elicit near-perfect agreement by giving 9/10 or 10/10 identical
responses. While the remaining cases show occasional indecision, models tend to predominantly give
the same recommendation when asked the same question twice. Although this consistency may be
reassuring for any individual patient, it comes at a structural cost.

Models do not track physician disagreement. Sorensen et al. [b] define distributional pluralism
as calibration of a model’s output distribution to a reference population. On a case where physicians
split 55–45, a pluralistic model should produce responses in roughly those proportions. Collectively
physicians disagree strongly, with high decision entropy across our panel for individual cases
(N = 20, median = 0.881, IQR = [0.610, 0.971]), while model decision entropy is near zero. Since
models make consistent decisions, they do not represent this range of reasonable disagreement.
If models retained even a weak form of distributional pluralism, their decision entropy should be
higher on cases where physicians are more divided. We test this for each model individually by
computing the Spearman correlation between physician consensus entropy and that model’s per-case
decision entropy across the benchmark. No individual model achieves a statistically significant
correlation (all p > 0.17), and per-model correlations are uniformly near zero (mean ρ = −0.021,
range [−0.18, 0.20]; Appendix E.1). As their uncertainty distribution is uncorrelated with physician
disagreement, models individually fail case level distributional pluralism.
````

E.2   Phrasing invariance

We select three frontier models for testing of phrasing invariance (Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.2,
Gemini 3.1 Pro) and run the experiment on all cases.

Rephrasing construction. Each vignette is decomposed (GPT-5.2, T =0) into an ethically inert
scaffold and one value phrase per engaged value. For each value phrase we generate 10 paraphrases
at five graded intensity levels (targeting 20%–100% of words changed). Each paraphrase is reinserted
into the scaffold with all other value phrases held constant. A reversed control flips the ethical valence
of each value phrase (e.g., a patient who prefers treatment A is described as preferring treatment B),
serving as a positive control reflecting value change rather than surface rephrasing. An identical retest
baseline provides the reference flip rate.

Decision trials. Each (variant, model) pair is evaluated over R=10 independent trials at T =1.0
with structured JSON output constraining responses to Choice 1 or Choice 2.

Flip rate. For each (case, model) pair, the baseline majority is the decision chosen in >50% of
retest trials; ties are excluded. A variant’s flip rate is the fraction of its R trials that deviate from this
majority; the flip delta ∆ is the difference between a variant’s flip rate and the retest flip rate.

Statistical tests. Spearman rank correlation between paraphrase intensity and flip rate yields
ρ=0.046 (p=0.002), a statistically detectable but negligible trend (ρ2 <0.003). A Mann–Whitney
