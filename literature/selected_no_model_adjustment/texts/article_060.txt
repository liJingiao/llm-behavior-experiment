                                Abstract
            The widespread success of large language mod-
            els (LLMs) on NLP benchmarks has been ac-
            companied by concerns that LLMs function pri-
            marily as stochastic parrots that reproduce texts
            similar to what they saw during pre-training,
            often erroneously. But what is the nature of
            their errors, and do these errors exhibit any
            regularities? In this work, we examine irrele-
            vant context hallucinations, in which models
            integrate misleading contextual cues into their
            predictions. Through behavioral analysis, we
            show that these errors result from a structured
            yet flawed mechanism that we term class-based
            (mis)generalization, in which models combine
            abstract class cues with features extracted from
            the query or context to derive answers. Fur-
